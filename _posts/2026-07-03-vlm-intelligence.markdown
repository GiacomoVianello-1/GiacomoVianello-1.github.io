abstract: "This thesis investigates the spatial reasoning capabilities of compact Vision-Language Models (VLMs) onboard resource-constrained UAVs for autonomous open-vocabulary Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav). We introduce a dual control strategy: a reactive baseline scoring candidate heading directions, and a probabilistic semantic framework that fuses VLM detections into a recursive Bayesian belief map coupled with an information-theoretic planner. To overcome altitude limitations beyond RGB-D sensing range, a multi-view triangulation module resolves monocular depth ambiguities. Evaluated in simulation and validated on a physical quadrotor, the probabilistic framework increases navigation success rates from 26.7% to 66.7% (reaching 95% on extended missions), complemented by an onboard benchmark of lightweight VLMs."
