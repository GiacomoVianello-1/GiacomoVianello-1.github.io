abstract: "This thesis investigates the spatial reasoning capabilities of compact Vision-Language Models (VLMs) onboard resource-constrained UAVs for autonomous open-vocabulary Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav). We propose two complementary control policies: a reactive baseline scoring candidate heading directions, and an advanced probabilistic semantic framework fusing VLM detections into a Bayesian belief map with an information-theoretic planner. To handle high-altitude flights beyond standard RGB-D ranges, a 3D target estimation module resolves monocular depth ambiguities through triangulation. Evaluated in simulation and on a physical quadrotor, the probabilistic framework increases mission success rates from 26.7% to 66.7% (reaching 95% on extended missions), complemented by a benchmark of lightweight VLMs as onboard decision-makers."

Master's thesis developed at the **University of Zaragoza (RoPeRT)** in collaboration with the **University of Padua**. The project explores deploying compact **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)** onboard drones for autonomous, open-vocabulary object search and navigation, combining probabilistic semantic mapping, active exploration planning, and real-world quadrotor flight validation.
